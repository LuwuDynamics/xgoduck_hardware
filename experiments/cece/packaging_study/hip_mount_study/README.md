# Cece V4 right hip: exact fit coupon and retention audit

The V4 body already has a **positive, serviceable screw-retained hip-servo bay**. Its right side has two molded Ø2.0 mm screw pilots at X=25.5 mm, Y=+8.7 and −19.5 mm, 28.2 mm apart. The intended method is to fasten the two SG90 mounting ears to these pilots, then use a supplied plastic horn as the leg drive. It uses the existing two hip and two ankle servos; it adds no motor. The Rapid Robotics listing states a 23.0 × 12.2 × 29.0 mm overall SG90 and supplies horns and accessories, but does not dimension the ear-hole pitch, screw sizes, output shaft, or actual horn. Therefore **do not declare this joint fit-verified yet**.

`Cece_Right_Hip_Fit_Coupon.stl` is a direct crop of the current V4 body, not a redrawn approximation. It preserves the entire right SG90 bay, both screw pilots, surrounding wall, and output opening (crop bounds X=14..38, Y=−29..16, Z=−39..−10 mm). It is a single watertight solid of 5296.397 mm³, about 6.57 g of solid PLA before supports and slicer settings. The FreeCAD source is `Cece_Right_Hip_Fit_Coupon.FCStd`; `build_coupon.py` regenerates it from the V4 body.

`check_coupon.py` and `coupon_check.json` record a nominal static interference check against the current V4 body, right ankle V2 leg, right wide foot, and latest rotated battery/UBEC clearance boxes. The coupon, leg, wide foot, hip case proxy, battery and UBEC have **zero pairwise solid overlap** for the selected assembly pairs. A Ø4 mm output-shaft proxy clears the body and leg, but overlaps the hip case box at its origin as expected. A *hypothetical* 3 × 15 × 2 mm straight horn arm at Z=−43 mm clears the body but **overlaps the printed leg by 11.963 mm³**. The actual supplied horn geometry and seated depth are unknown; this is a physical fit gate, not evidence that the horn will seat.

## One-hip trial before a complete print

1. Print the coupon in the same PLA, orientation, and tolerances intended for the body. Use one of the SG90s actually purchased from Rapid Robotics.
2. Measure the mounting-ear hole spacing, hole diameter, case width, output-shaft centre relative to the ears, and the supplied screw lengths. Check that both ear holes align with the 28.2 mm pilot pitch without bending the ears or forcing screws. Check screw engagement and that the case remains removable.
3. Centre the servo electrically at 90° without a horn. Test-fit the supplied horn in the current right leg's cross slot, checking width, seating depth, spline engagement, and centre retaining screw. Trim only the excess horn arms after confirming the chosen orientation. Do not glue the horn to compensate for an ill-fitting slot.
4. Assemble the coupon, servo and leg. Check full hand movement with power disconnected, then move slowly through ±20° at a correctly rated 5 V supply. Stop if the case shifts, the horn slips, the plastic cracks, or a cable rubs.
5. If any dimension fails, measure the offending feature and revise the bay or leg slot from that measurement before printing the full body. A photo with calipers beside the actual servo and supplied horn would be sufficient to update the CAD accurately.

These mesh checks cover nominal neutral-pose geometry only. They do not establish ear-hole fit, pilot grip, cable bend, print shrinkage, gait loads, bearing wear, or walking stability. Sources: [purchased Rapid Robotics SG90](https://rapidroboticsaustralia.com.au/products/sg90-micro-servo), [SG90 dimensional sheet](https://static.rapidonline.com/pdf/37-1330t.pdf).
